Build the Feature Extractor

Your job: find out what each component of a CNN does by changing it and watching what happens. First, run one 3×3 filter over a real mammogram patch. Then design a whole feature extractor, break it on purpose, and compare your designs with VGG19, the network behind the case study's 25,088 features.

Nothing pinned yet. Pin VGG19 first, then change one component and pin again.

Try this

  1. Load VGG19 and pin it. Where do the 25,088 features come from? Which single layer holds most of the parameters?
  2. Switch the activation to none. Why does a 16-conv network now do no better than one layer?
  3. Turn pooling off. Watch the receptive field and the compute. Click a grid cell: can one feature still see the whole mass?
  4. Try 7 × 7 kernels. The receptive field grows, so why does test accuracy drop?
  5. Set padding to none with 7 × 7 kernels and 5 blocks. What happens to the map, and why does almost every real CNN pad?
  6. Swap the VGG head for the small head. What happens to the parameter count and to the train–test gap?

After the workshop: a short Slido quiz asks what you saw when you changed each component. Pinned designs are the best notes to keep.
