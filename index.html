Hands-on CNN workshops

Three short, interactive pages from the lecture. Change a component and watch what it does to the model, test whether a heatmap can be trusted, and build a melanoma app that has to work for everyone.

  • Runs in your browser
  • No install, no login
  • Laptop recommended; phones work
  • Nothing you do leaves your device
  1. Workshop 1 · about 11 min

    Design your own feature extractor

    You will
    Pick a real mammogram patch, then build a CNN one locked choice at a time: filter size, padding, activation, pooling, depth and the classifier head, seeing each one on your patch.
    You will learn
    What each component does to the model’s size, what one feature can see, and its accuracy.
    Open workshop 1 →
  2. Workshop 2 · about 11 min

    Where is the model looking?

    You will
    Race the clock on four unlabeled mammogram patches: mass or no mass, and where a model should look. Then compare with the model’s calls and heatmaps, test one by covering its hot spot, and meet a second model with the same accuracy.
    You will learn
    Why a heatmap shows where the evidence came from, not that the model is right.
    Open workshop 2 →
  3. Workshop 3 · teams of 2–3

    Build the best melanoma app

    You will
    Spend a token budget on a CNN, training photos, cleaning and a decision threshold, then deploy your app to a city.
    You will learn
    How the training data decides who an accurate model still misses.
    Open workshop 3 →

Good to know

  • Real and simulated parts are labeled on each page. Workshop 1’s shapes, parameter counts and receptive fields are exact; its accuracy is a teaching simulation. Workshop 2’s heatmaps and model scores are real; covered-patch scores are simulated. Workshop 3 uses synthetic data.
  • After Workshops 1 and 2 there is a short Slido quiz on what you saw. Workshop 3 uses Slido polls and a code word from the slides.
  • Keyboard and screen readers: every control is a standard button or input you can reach with Tab. Pages follow your system’s light or dark mode.
